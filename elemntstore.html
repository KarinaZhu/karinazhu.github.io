ELEMNT.STORE

A focused redesign exploration for an independent audio brand — improving product visibility and purchase hierarchy without flattening its bold visual identity.

ScopeHomepage · Product Page
RoleUI/UX Designer
StatusRedesign exploration · prepared for founder review

01 — The Brief

Improve product visibility and support conversion while protecting the brand's experimental visual language.

Context

ELEMNT.STORE is an independent audio brand selling sample packs and sound products for music producers. The existing site already has a distinctive visual identity: fluorescent green, bold campaign imagery and a strong editorial attitude.

The redesign does not aim to make the brand more generic or “clean.” The goal is to improve how people move from campaign discovery to product understanding and purchase.

02 — Current Experience Audit

The visual identity is already strong. The opportunity is in hierarchy, scanability and purchase confidence.

Homepage

Campaign impact is high, but the catalogue becomes visually repetitive.

Product cards share similar visual weight and limited supporting information, so differences between products may be harder to understand without opening multiple pages.

Design hypothesis

Product page

Rich information competes instead of following one clear decision hierarchy.

The page contains audio, purchase options, genre information, descriptions, bundle contents and artist context. The redesign can make the sequence easier to scan.

Observation

Purchase cues

Audio preview and “what's included” can work harder earlier in the journey.

For a sound product, hearing the product and understanding its contents are core decision cues. Their prominence should be tested rather than assumed.

03 — Design Goals

Preserve the attitude. Clarify the shopping experience.

01

Keep ELEMNT.STORE recognisable. The redesign should retain the fluorescent colour, oversized graphics and music-culture energy.

02

Make the catalogue easier to scan by strengthening hierarchy and giving products clearer context before the click.

03

Make the product page answer the purchase questions in a more deliberate order: hear it, understand it, then buy it.

04 — Homepage Redesign

Move from one strong campaign moment to a clearer sequence of discovery.

Prototype direction

The homepage redesign keeps the large campaign hero, but introduces stronger separation between new releases, featured products and the wider catalogue.

Product cards can carry more useful context — such as format, sound category or a short descriptor — while still allowing artwork to remain the dominant visual element.

01

Campaign Hero

Keep the bold drop-focused opening. One primary CTA, one clear release message.

02

Featured Drop

Give the newest or highest-priority product a stronger editorial block before the catalogue.

03

Product Discovery

Use clearer grouping and more informative cards so users can scan differences faster.

04

Brand / Artist Story

Use selected editorial content to reinforce credibility without interrupting shopping.

05 — Product Page Redesign

For an audio product, the page should help users hear, understand and compare before asking them to purchase.

Prototype direction

The redesign can bring the audio preview, key product information and purchase options into a stronger first viewport, then move deeper details into a clearer progressive hierarchy.

Artist story and visual content remain important, but should support the buying decision rather than compete with it.

01

Product Identity

Artwork, title, price, genre and a short descriptor establish the product immediately.

02

Audio Preview

Make listening feel like a primary product interaction, not a secondary detail.

03

What's Included

Summarise samples, presets or bundle contents before the long-form description.

04

Purchase Choice

Keep edition / bundle options understandable and visually close to the add-to-cart action.

06 — Prototype

A short prototype is enough: campaign → discovery → product → preview → cart.

Suggested flow

The prototype does not need to reproduce the entire store. A focused five-step flow can show the redesign logic more clearly than a large set of disconnected screens.

For founder review, I would prototype one homepage path into one representative product page and finish at add-to-cart.

07 — Founder Review

The next step is feedback from the people closest to the business.

This redesign is intentionally framed as an exploration until the founder has reviewed it.

After review, this section can be updated with founder feedback, implementation decisions and — if available — real performance data. Until then, the case study separates observations from hypotheses and avoids claiming conversion improvements that have not been measured.

08 — Reflection

The design challenge is not to make a strong brand quieter. It is to make the shopping logic as intentional as the visual identity.

Takeaway

This project is an opportunity to practise designing within an existing brand system rather than replacing it. The redesign focuses on prioritisation, product hierarchy and e-commerce decision-making while preserving the character that makes ELEMNT.STORE distinctive.
