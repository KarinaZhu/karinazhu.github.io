Project 01 · UI/UX Design · Product Concept

Pilates Circle

A mobile-first Pilates discovery experience designed to make finding, comparing and booking classes feel clearer and more personal.

RoleFounder · Product Designer
FocusUser research · UX strategy · UI design · prototyping
StatusOngoing product development
Hero imagePilates Circle.jpg

01 — The Challenge

Pilates discovery is visually fragmented, and the experience often becomes harder than the class itself.

The project began by looking at the experience around choosing a class: browsing different studios, comparing options and moving between discovery and booking.

The design goal was to create a calmer, more coherent experience that reduces unnecessary decision friction while keeping users in control.

02 — User Research

Research informed the interface, but selected findings remain private.

I spoke with Pilates users at different experience levels, alongside an instructor, to understand how people currently discover classes and what affects confidence during the decision process.

Those conversations shaped the product structure, information hierarchy and interaction flow. Detailed research findings and product logic have been intentionally omitted from this public case study.

03 — Design Direction

Reduce complexity before adding more choice.

The experience uses a lightweight onboarding flow to create a more relevant starting point, rather than asking users to navigate a dense interface from the beginning.

From there, users can explore nearby options, move between views and continue into class details and booking without losing context.

04 — Discovery Experience

A flexible browse experience across map, list and studio detail.

The discovery experience was designed around clarity and continuity. Users can switch between spatial and list-based views, refine what they see and move into a studio profile without restarting the journey.

Studio pages use strong imagery, concise information and clear calls to action to help users understand the experience quickly.

05 — Booking Flow

Keep the journey consistent from discovery to confirmation.

The booking flow was designed to preserve context while users review a class, create an account, complete payment and receive confirmation.

The focus was not on adding more steps, but on making each transition feel predictable and visually connected to the experience that came before it.

06 — Product Direction

The public case study shows the interface — not the full product strategy.

Pilates Circle is an active product concept with additional research, recommendation logic and future product layers that are not published here.

This portfolio focuses only on selected UI/UX decisions: onboarding, discovery, studio detail, booking and account flows.

Confidentiality note

Selected product logic, research findings and future features have been intentionally omitted.

The project is still evolving, and this public case study is designed to show my product design process without revealing proprietary strategy.

07 — Reflection

The strongest experience came from simplifying what users see — not exposing everything the product knows.

Pilates Circle helped me think more carefully about progressive disclosure, information hierarchy and how to design a recommendation-led experience without making the interface feel complicated.

The project continues to evolve alongside ongoing user research and product development.
